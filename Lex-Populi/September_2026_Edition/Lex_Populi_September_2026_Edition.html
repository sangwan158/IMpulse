BRICS Summit 2026: India Leads the Global South

2 min read

Tap or click the infographic to enlarge.

Overview

India hosted the 18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi on 12-13 September 2026, its fourth time chairing the bloc. Held under the theme “Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability”, the summit brought together all 11 members: Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa, Egypt, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Together they account for about 49.5% of the world’s population, 40% of global GDP and 26% of global trade. The summit capped a year of 400+ meetings held across 30 Indian cities.

Key Points

  1. New Delhi Declaration: A 140-point declaration was adopted unanimously, backed by 50+ concrete initiatives, networks and action plans.
  2. Local-Currency Trade: The BRICS Payment Task Force was mandated to connect member payment systems so that trade can be settled in local currencies.
  3. Financial Safety Net: Amendments to the Contingent Reserve Arrangement strengthen crisis support without relying on the IMF.
  4. Food Security: BRICS aims to launch a Grain Exchange by 2027 to reduce grain price volatility.
  5. Technology and DPI: The bloc will build a BRICS Digital Public Infrastructure repository and an expanded satellite constellation, and will study a direct submarine data cable.
  6. Counter-Terrorism: Leaders condemned cross-border terrorism and the 2025 Pahalgam attack.

Global Significance

The summit showed India as a consensus-builder at a divided moment. Despite sharp differences among members over the wars in Iran and Ukraine, India secured a unanimous declaration. China and Russia explicitly backed India’s and Brazil’s aspirations for a greater role in a reformed UN Security Council. India also proposed a 10-point roadmap to reform global governance built on the “3Rs”: Representation, Responsiveness and Rule-making. The bloc jointly opposed unilateral carbon border taxes such as the EU’s CBAM, a major concern for Indian exporters. China will host the 19th summit in 2027.

Impact on India

  • GIFT City: Will host the BRICS Risk Lab, boosting Gujarat’s reinsurance and financial hub ambitions.
  • MSMEs: The Jaipur Consensus will study invoice discounting to unlock working capital for small businesses.
  • Health: NIMHANS will lead a BRICS mental health training network, and AMTZ in Andhra Pradesh hosts the new BRICS BioDesign Centre.
  • Trade: Local-currency settlement can reduce dollar dependence and currency risk for Indian exporters.

SEMICON India 2026: From Silicon to Systems

2 min read

Tap or click the infographic to enlarge.

Overview

The fifth edition of SEMICON India ran from 17-19 September 2026 at Yashobhoomi (IICC), New Delhi. It was jointly organised by SEMI and the India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) on the theme “Silicon to Systems: Building the Ecosystem”, and was inaugurated by PM Narendra Modi on Vishwakarma Jayanti. With 51,656 registrations and participants from 52 countries, it was the largest edition yet. Its new “Value Chain Experience” exhibit walked visitors through India’s entire chip journey, from sand to silicon to finished systems.

Key Points

  1. Semicon 2.0: A second phase worth ₹1,27,500 crore (about $13.5 bn), focused on six areas: design, machines and materials, new fabs, advanced packaging, research and talent.
  2. Production Goes Live: The PM virtually opened commercial production lines at CDIL Semiconductor, Mohali and Suchi Semicon, Surat.
  3. 56 Announcements: MoUs and initiatives across chip design, fabrication, packaging, AI, high-performance computing and skilling.
  4. Homegrown Chips: The Digital India RISC-V Grand Challenge drew 1,236 teams. Winners built a water-cleaning robot and a digital stethoscope on Indian processors (SHAKTI and VEGA).
  5. Indigenous Computing: C-DAC launched PARAM Vidya, an AI lab-in-a-box for universities, and PARAM Shavak-QS, a compact quantum simulator.

Global Significance

Semiconductors power everything from smartphones and EVs to AI and defence systems, yet global chip production is concentrated in a handful of East Asian economies. SEMICON India 2026 marked India’s shift from policy announcements to actual commercial production, strengthening its pitch as a trusted, diversified partner in global supply chains. Participation from 52 countries and new international tie-ups, such as the UK-backed Bharat AI-SoC Challenge, show growing global confidence in India’s chip ambitions.

Impact on India

  • Manufacturing: 5 of the 12 chip units approved under Semicon 1.0 are now in commercial production.
  • Market Growth: India’s chip market could grow from about $64 bn in 2026 to $200 bn by 2035 (EY-IESA report).
  • Self-Reliance: Domestic chips can cut India’s electronics import bill and supply risk for phones, EVs and defence.
  • Jobs and Skills: Demand is rising for engineers in chip design, fab processes, equipment and packaging, supported by new skilling tie-ups.
  • Startups: The Chips to Startup programme gives students and young firms a path from design to product.

Indian Economy in Q2 2026: Growth Holds Firm Amid a Global Storm

2 min read

Tap or click the infographic to enlarge.

Overview

Data released by MoSPI on 31 August 2026 shows India’s real GDP grew 7.8% in April to June 2026 (Q1 of FY 2026-27), rising to ₹81.36 lakh crore from ₹75.46 lakh crore a year ago. Growth beat the RBI’s 7% estimate and the 7.1% expected by economists. Gross Value Added (GVA), a cleaner measure of core activity, grew 8.2%. The previous quarter’s growth was revised up to 8.6%. This performance came despite an oil price shock triggered by the conflict in the Middle East.

Key Points

  1. Manufacturing: Grew 9.2%, up from 8.3% a year ago.
  2. Financial Services: Grew 12.1%, up from 8.8%, driven by strong bank lending.
  3. Utilities and Services: Electricity and utilities grew 8.9%, and trade, hotels, transport and communication grew 8.5%.
  4. Weak Spots: Agriculture slowed to 3.6%, while mining contracted 2.4%.
  5. Demand: Private consumption grew 7.1% and private investment nearly 12%. Bank credit growth hit 18.3%, the highest in over a decade.

Global Context

India remains the fastest-growing G20 economy. However, the war in the Middle East pushed up energy prices and put heavy pressure on the rupee, which hovered near ₹96 per dollar in September. Instead of raising interest rates, the RBI used targeted tools such as a special FCNR(B) deposit swap window, helping attract over $41 billion in capital inflows. India’s trade deficit also narrowed to $26.9 billion in August.

Impact at Home

  • Tax Relief Working: GST and income tax cuts earlier in the year have lifted household spending.
  • Inflation Rising: CPI crossed the RBI’s 4% target in June for the first time in 16 months and reached 4.82% in August, the highest since December 2024, led by food and fuel.
  • RBI Stance: In August, the RBI kept the repo rate at 5.25% with a neutral stance, raised its FY27 growth forecast to 6.7% and projected FY27 inflation at 5.0%.
  • What to Watch: Whether rising prices push the RBI towards a rate hike. July to September GDP data is due on 30 November 2026.

Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act 2026: A 100% Tariff Threat for India

3 min read

Tap or click the infographic to enlarge.

Overview

On 18 September 2026, the US President signed H.R. 5334, the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, into law. The Act sets up a framework of sanctions and tariffs to cut off funding for Russia’s war in Ukraine and extends the Iran Sanctions Act of 1996 until 2031. It cleared the Senate 86-11 on 7 August and the House 262-159 on 16 September. For India, one of the world’s largest buyers of Russian crude, it turns a diplomatic balancing act into a direct trade risk.

Key Points

  1. 100% Tariff on Oil Buyers: Up to 100% duty on all goods from countries that keep buying Russian oil or gas after 18 October 2026 and rank among its top five importers or sanctions-evasion facilitators.
  2. 500% Tariff on Russia: A separate duty of up to 500% on goods imported from Russia itself.
  3. Shadow Fleet Sanctions: Tankers carrying Russian energy, along with their owners, insurers and port operators, face blocking sanctions.
  4. Banking Curbs: Measures target Russia’s central bank, Sberbank, VTB, Gazprombank and foreign banks that deal with them.
  5. Presidential Waivers: Tariffs can be waived in the US national interest, and the top-five list is reviewed every 180 days.

Global Significance

The Act shifts US pressure from specific defence deals, as under CAATSA (2017), to entire trade flows. It tests India’s strategic autonomy, since India follows only UN-mandated sanctions. On 17 September, the MEA said India will protect its energy security through diversified sourcing. Any new duty would come on top of the existing 10% Section 301 tariff and 50% Section 232 tariffs on steel, aluminium and copper, and could delay India-US trade deal talks. India has won carve-outs before, such as for Chabahar Port, so seeking a waiver is likely to be its first move.

Impact on India

  • Energy Security: Russia supplied over 30% of India’s crude imports in FY26 (about $40.8 bn) and over 40% in August 2026. Replacing it within weeks is difficult.
  • Fuel and Inflation: Costlier crude would push up fuel, transport and fertiliser prices.
  • Exports at Risk: India sold $87.31 bn of goods to the US in FY26, led by electrical machinery, pharmaceuticals, textiles and gems and jewellery.
  • Rupee Pressure: Lower exports and a higher oil bill could widen the current account deficit and weaken the rupee.
  • What to Watch: The 18 October trigger date and whether India secures a waiver.
